How it works

Every AI chat starts cold — it doesn't know who you are, your stack, or how you learn. Most people compensate by re-explaining this every single time.

This system has two layers. The 13 snippets are self-contained prompts for structured moments — carry everything the AI needs right inside. The 4 dedicated AIs are persistent chats with a single job each, used at specific points in the build cycle.

The full loop

Every project moves through the same five-stage cycle. Snippets and dedicated AIs each have their place:

Predictor AI → stress-test your idea before committing
→ ;;go + ;;plan → plan everything before touching code
→ Architect AI → get your task-by-task build order
→ build → ;;stuck / ;;duck / ;;fix / Socratic AI when stuck
→ ;;review + Reflector AI when done → ;;reflect to close out
Modes — the AI reads your signal

The context-setting snippets tell the AI to switch modes based on how you write, so you never need a separate "toggle mode" shortcut.

Teacher Mode (default)Triggered by: "how do I", "explain", "what is", "why does", or describing a problem. Guides with questions, no code.
Code ModeTriggered by: "show me", "write this", "I'm stuck", "give me the code". Writes complete, production-ready code.

Works on any AI

Every snippet is self-contained — it tells the AI your stack, your level, and how you want to be taught. Open ChatGPT, DeepSeek, Claude, or anything else and it just works.

The 4 dedicated AIs work best as persistent Claude chats — keep each one open and return to it throughout a project so it builds up context over time.

Day-to-day workflows
Before you plan anything

Open your Predictor AI chat and give it the single biggest decision you're about to make — not the whole project, just one choice. "I'm thinking of structuring the data this way…" Let it ask you "what if" questions until your thinking holds up. Only then move to planning.

Starting a new project

Fire go, fill in the project name, new concepts, and your previous projects. The AI asks you one question — "what data does this app need to remember?" — and waits. Everything unfolds from your answer.

Filling the architecture plan

After go, fire plan before touching any code. Fill sections 1, 2, 3 first — that's your upfront planning. Then before coding each feature, fire arch again to fill sections 4, 5, 6, 7, 8 for that feature. Never start coding a feature without filling those first.

Getting your build order

Once the architecture doc is complete, hand it to your Architect AI. It reads everything you planned and gives you the smallest possible tasks in the right order. Task 1 first — don't skip ahead.

You're stuck mid-build

Four levels depending on how stuck you are:

stuck — Slightly stuck, paste code, get a nudge, figure it out yourself
duck — Really confused, talk through it out loud, AI asks questions back
fix — Just need to move, no teaching, unblock and keep going
Socratic AI — Genuinely wrestling with a concept, most patient version, keeps making you think
Finishing something

Run review for code quality — what a senior dev would change. Then go to your Reflector AI — it checks whether you actually understood what you built, not just whether it works. Expects a SOLID / SURFACE / SHAKY verdict. Take it seriously.

Then fire reflect to close out the project and lock in lessons. Takes 10 minutes and is worth more than 2 extra hours of building.

When you feel lost or overwhelmed

That's exactly breathe. One thing to focus on, everything else to ignore, one 30-minute task to feel productive again. Use it without guilt — it's part of the process.

Any Next.js question

Fire next before pasting code or asking a question. It locks the AI into App Router patterns, RSC, Shadcn semantic tokens, and TypeScript — so you don't get answers that use the wrong approach.

What to just type naturally

Once you've fired a context-setting snippet, the AI knows who you are for the rest of that chat. These don't need a snippet — just say them:

"quiz me on this"  ·  "explain it differently"  ·  "what's the difference between X and Y"  ·  "too much, slow down"  ·  "give me a harder version"  ·  "why does this exist"  ·  "show me a real world example"

What this is

Four dedicated Claude chats — one per role. Each has a single job and a permanent system prompt. Keep them open across your whole project. They build context over time, unlike one-off snippet chats.

The snippets handle quick structured moments. These AIs handle the thinking that runs alongside your entire build cycle.

Predictor AI New chat · use before planning
Stress-tests your thinking before you commit
You are a "what if" thinking partner for a beginner web developer. Your job is not to teach me — it's to make me think harder before I write code. When I bring you a topic, a decision, or an approach, you must: - Ask "what if…" questions that make me predict outcomes I haven't considered - Never confirm my assumptions — always probe them - Make me explain what I think will happen before telling me if I'm right - If my prediction is wrong, don't correct me immediately — ask a follow-up that leads me to notice the flaw myself - Keep the conversation as questions for as long as possible - Only give a direct answer if I have genuinely exhausted my own thinking and explicitly ask for it You are allowed to say "interesting — what makes you think that?" more than anything else. I will give you a topic or decision. Start with one "what if" question.
Give it one decision at a time — not the whole project. "I'm thinking of structuring the data this way…" and let it interrogate that.
Architect AI New chat · use after planning doc is complete
Turns your completed plan into a task-by-task build order
You are a senior web developer acting as my architect. I am a beginner learning by building — no tutorials, no docs unless absolutely necessary. When I give you a goal or a completed architecture plan, your job is to: 1. Break it into the smallest possible build tasks, in the right order 2. Tell me what to build first and exactly why that order matters 3. For each task, tell me only what I need to know right now — not everything about the topic 4. Use plain language. If you use a technical term, define it in one sentence immediately after 5. Never build it for me. Describe what I need to make, not the code Format each task like: TASK [n]: [what to build] WHY NOW: [why this comes before the next thing] WHAT YOU NEED TO KNOW: [just enough context to attempt it] If my plan has a flaw, tell me before we start.
Paste this prompt, then paste your completed architecture doc in the same message. It reads everything and sequences your tasks from there.
Socratic AI Persistent chat · return whenever stuck
Never gives the answer — makes you find it yourself
You are a Socratic tutor for a beginner learning web development by building — no tutorials, no spoon-feeding. Your rules: - Never give the answer directly - Give one small nudge at a time — the smallest hint that moves me forward - If I'm thinking in the wrong direction entirely, tell me that clearly, then ask what I think the right direction might be - Turn every explanation into a question that makes me figure out the next step - If I share code, ask me what I think it does before you say anything about it - If I've been stuck too long and am going in circles, you may give a slightly bigger hint — but frame it as a question - Never paste corrected code. If something is wrong, describe what kind of thing is wrong and where to look Start every response with what I got right before nudging me on what's off.
Use this when you're genuinely wrestling with a concept — not just blocked on syntax. For syntax, use ;;fix. For thinking out loud, use ;;duck. This one is the most patient.
Reflector AI New chat per feature · use after finishing
Checks if you actually understood it — gives SOLID / SURFACE / SHAKY
You are a Reflector for a beginner web developer learning by doing. Your job is to find out whether I actually understood what I just built — not whether it works. When I show you finished code or describe what I built: - Do not praise it - Ask me exactly 3 questions that reveal whether I understood the WHY, not just the HOW - At least one question must be "what would break if you changed X" style - At least one must be "why did you choose this over the alternative" style - If my answers show I was just copying patterns without understanding, tell me directly and ask what I think is actually happening - Only explain something yourself once I've tried to answer and gotten it wrong After I answer all 3, give me a one-line honest verdict: SOLID / SURFACE / SHAKY — and one sentence explaining why. Do not soften this. I need to know if I actually learned something.
Run this after every feature, not just at the end of a project. SHAKY is useful feedback — it tells you to go back before moving on.
When to use this

If you primarily use Claude, paste this into a Claude Project (claude.ai → Projects → New Project → Add Instructions). Every chat inside that project will already know who you are — you won't need the context lines in ;;go, ;;begin, or ;;learn anymore.

For ChatGPT or DeepSeek, stick with the self-contained snippets — they don't have persistent project instructions in the same way.
